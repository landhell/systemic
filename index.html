陈威汕是一位现居吉隆坡的艺术工作者与牧者，创作以绘画为主，灵感源自自然生命体的系统性与秩序。他透过化约的视觉语言，运用色块、线条与圆点，探索生命在变化与结构之间的存在状态。

Chan Wei San is a Malaysian artist and pastor based in Kuala Lumpur. His painting practice is inspired by living systems in nature, exploring the relationship between order and change through reductive visual language. Using color, lines, and dots, his works investigate structure, rhythm, and the systemic beauty of life.

《Systemic》系列作品，源于艺术家对自然生命形式的持续观察与感知，试图从局部理解整体。陈威汕在创作中提出的问题是：世界是如何存在的？它是自然而然生成的，还是被创造的秩序？从艺术工作者的视角，我们能否仅以“机遇”与“进化”来解释生命的复杂与精密？

在他看来，自然界的生命既柔和有机，又显现出高度的系统性；变化万千之中自有其秩序。生命体的色彩、线条、图案与肌理之美，仿佛蕴含着精密的设计痕迹。

在《Systemic》中，陈威汕以化约的视觉语言与模仿手法，运用色块、线条与圆点，建构出一种超越具象形态的结构之美。他追求的，不是对象的再现，而是源自“创造行为本身”的原初之美。

系列创作亦受到英文集体名词的启发，如 a colony of virus、an eclipse of moths、a bloom of jellyfish。这些词不仅描述数量，更传达系统的行为、节奏与特性。每一个“殖民”、“蚀影”或“盛放”都成为积累、流动与互动的微观世界，呈现出脆弱却强大的秩序模式。

透过作品，艺术家邀请观者重新观看自然——意识到大自然与生命体并非随机演化的产物，而是充满秩序、关系与设计的精密系统。当世界被理解为受造之物，这本身即成为一种知识与觉醒。

The Systemic series originates from the artist’s close observation and perception of living forms in nature, seeking to understand the whole from the part. Chan Wei San poses questions that guide his work: How does the world exist? Is it simply a product of chance and evolution, or is there inherent order? From an artist’s perspective, can life be explained solely by opportunity and adaptation?

To him, life in nature is both gentle and organic, yet profoundly systemic. Amid constant change, a hidden order emerges. The colors, lines, patterns, and textures of living forms suggest a subtle yet precise design.

In Systemic, Chan employs reductive visual language and mimetic strategies, using blocks of color, lines, and dots to construct forms that transcend representation. His goal is not to reproduce objects, but to evoke a primordial beauty rooted in the very act of creation.

The series is also inspired by the evocative nature of English collective nouns, such as a colony of virus, an eclipse of moths, or a bloom of jellyfish. These expressions do more than quantify; they describe the behavior, rhythm, and character of systems. Each “colony,” “eclipse,” or “bloom” becomes a microcosm of accumulation, movement, and interaction, revealing patterns that are both fragile and powerful.
